# 高考英语典型真题精选

## Part 1: Grammar and Vocabulary

### Question 1: Tense
It _______ ten years since we last met.
A. was  B. has been  C. is  D. will be

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:**
- "It has been + 时间段 + since..." 固定句型
- 表示"自从...已经多久了"
- 用现在完成时

**考点**: 现在完成时、固定句型

---

### Question 2: Non-finite Verb
The building _______ now will be our new library.
A. built  B. to be built  C. being built  D. having been built

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:**
- now提示正在进行
- building与build是被动关系
- being built表示"正在被建造"

**考点**: 非谓语动词、被动语态

---

### Question 3: Subjunctive Mood
If I _______ you, I would accept the offer.
A. am  B. was  C. were  D. had been

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:**
- 与现在事实相反的虚拟语气
- if从句用过去式
- be动词统一用were

**考点**: 虚拟语气

## Part 2: Reading Comprehension

**Passage A**

Artificial intelligence (AI) is transforming the way we live and work. From self-driving cars to medical diagnosis, AI applications are becoming increasingly common. However, this rapid development also raises important ethical questions.

One major concern is job displacement. As AI systems become more capable, they may replace human workers in many industries. Experts estimate that millions of jobs could be automated in the coming decades. This could lead to significant economic and social challenges.

Another issue is privacy. AI systems often require large amounts of personal data to function effectively. This raises questions about how this data is collected, stored, and used. There are concerns that AI could be used for surveillance or manipulation.

Despite these challenges, many researchers believe that AI can be developed responsibly. They argue that with proper regulation and ethical guidelines, AI can benefit society while minimizing potential harms.

**Questions:**

1. What is the main topic of the passage?
   A. The benefits of AI
   B. AI and ethical concerns
   C. How AI works
   D. The history of AI
   **Answer:** B

2. According to the passage, what is one major concern about AI?
   A. It's too expensive
   B. It may replace human jobs
   C. It's difficult to use
   D. It consumes too much energy
   **Answer:** B

3. What does "displacement" in paragraph 2 mean?
   A. Movement  B. Replacement  C. Training  D. Improvement
   **Answer:** B

4. What is mentioned about AI and privacy?
   A. AI protects privacy well
   B. AI doesn't need personal data
   C. AI may threaten privacy
   D. Privacy is not important for AI
   **Answer:** C

5. What is the author's attitude toward AI development?
   A. Completely negative
   B. Completely positive
   C. Neutral and balanced
   D. Indifferent
   **Answer:** C

## Part 3: Writing

**Task 1: Summary Writing (60 words)**

**Original Text:**
Online learning has become increasingly popular in recent years. It offers flexibility, allowing students to study at their own pace and schedule. However, it also has drawbacks. Some students find it difficult to stay motivated without face-to-face interaction. Technical problems can also disrupt learning.

**Sample Summary:**
Online learning provides flexibility for students to learn independently. However, it faces challenges including lack of motivation due to limited personal interaction and potential technical issues that may interrupt the learning process. (33 words)

---

**Task 2: Argumentative Essay (120-150 words)**

**Topic:** Should students be allowed to use smartphones in school?

**Sample Answer:**

In my opinion, students should be allowed to use smartphones in school, but with proper restrictions.

Smartphones can be valuable educational tools. They provide instant access to information, educational apps, and online resources that enhance learning. Students can use them for research, taking notes, and collaborating on projects.

However, unrestricted use can be problematic. Smartphones may distract students from lessons and reduce face-to-face communication. Therefore, schools should establish clear guidelines. For example, phones could be allowed during breaks and for specific educational purposes, but banned during classes unless instructed by teachers.

With balanced policies, smartphones can support rather than hinder education. The key is teaching students responsible usage rather than imposing complete bans.

(127 words)
